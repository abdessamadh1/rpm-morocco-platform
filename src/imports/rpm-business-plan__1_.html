SECTION 01

Executive Summary ORIGINAL

Moroccan private clinics earn from surgeries, not from occupied recovery beds — yet a nursing shortage and weak post-discharge care force them to keep patients hospitalized longer than needed.

PROBLEM 01

Severe nursing shortage

Too few trained nurses track too many in-hospital patients, so manual observation doesn't scale.

PROBLEM 02

Bed turnover friction

Clinics profit from active surgeries. Fear of post-op complications at home delays discharge and blocks profitable beds.

PROBLEM 03

Urban–rural care gap

Elite facilities cluster in Casablanca, Rabat and Marrakech. Rural patients get world-class surgery, then go home to zero follow-up infrastructure.

The solution

A B2B Hardware-as-a-Service + Software-as-a-Service platform. Clinics hand discharging patients a pre-configured cellular wearable. The device streams vital signs (heart rhythm, SpO₂, blood pressure) to our cloud in real time. The moment a reading crosses a clinical threshold, the clinic's on-duty nurse gets an instant dashboard alert and SMS.

Positioning: we do not build hardware and we do not deliver medical care. We are the data fabric — the pipe, the alarm, and the dashboard — that lets one desk nurse safely monitor up to 100 remote patients at once.

PLAIN TERMS

RPM (Remote Patient Monitoring): tracking a patient's vital signs from their home instead of a hospital bed.

HaaS / SaaS: the clinic never buys anything. It rents the devices and the software together for one monthly fee per patient — like leasing, not purchasing.

Telemetry: the continuous stream of measurements (heart rate, oxygen, blood pressure) a device sends automatically over the mobile network.

SECTION 03

Technical Architecture ORIGINAL

Healthcare telemetry is continuous and life-critical, so the backend skips fragile request/response web APIs in favor of an immutable, event-driven streaming pipeline.

Cellular OEM wearableEncrypted MQTT over 4G LTE-M — no Wi-Fi, no Bluetooth, no app. Works out of the box.
│ ▼
AWS IoT CoreSecure front door: authenticates every device and receives the encrypted packets.
│ ▼
Amazon MSK (Kafka)Immutable log absorbing millions of data points per minute without dropping a packet. 24-hour retention.
│ ▼
Alerting engine (Go/Rust)Watches the stream in real time. SpO₂ < 90% or an acute heart-rate spike bypasses the database and fires the WebSocket dashboard alert + emergency SMS (Twilio / Infobip) instantly.
│ ▼
Amazon EKS + ArgoCDAll services run containerized on Kubernetes, deployed by GitOps: zero-downtime updates, self-healing, automatic rollback.
│ ▼
Amazon S3 Standard-IAAfter evaluation, telemetry is bundled and archived to cheap, compliant long-term storage.
PLAIN TERMS

MQTT: a lightweight messaging language designed for small devices on weak networks — like SMS for machines, but constant and encrypted.

Kafka: a conveyor belt for data. Every reading is placed on the belt in order and nothing falls off, even at huge volume.

Kubernetes (EKS) + ArgoCD (GitOps): the software runs in identical containers; the desired state lives in Git, and the cluster continuously corrects itself to match it. If a server dies at 3 a.m., it heals without human intervention.

Why the alert "bypasses the database": writing to disk first adds seconds. For a patient whose oxygen is crashing, the alarm must fire in milliseconds — storage happens afterwards.

Added engineering requirement: alert delivery guarantees

ADDED  An alert that silently fails is a lawsuit. The pipeline must include: delivery acknowledgement (a nurse must tap "seen" within N minutes or the alert escalates to a second phone), a dead-man switch for devices that stop transmitting (no data for 15 min = its own alert type), and a monthly end-to-end fire-drill test per clinic.

SECTION 04

Hardware Procurement Strategy ORIGINAL

We never build hardware. We white-label medically certified devices from established Asian OEM/ODM factories and point their firmware at our cloud.

DEVICE CLASS A

Continuous ECG patches

e.g. Viatom / Wellue ER1 class — 18g clinical single-lead ECG worn on the chest, streaming heart data continuously for 24–72h.

DEVICE CLASS B

Cellular blood-pressure cuffs

e.g. Transtek TeleRPM class — familiar-looking home BP monitor with a built-in SIM that transmits each reading on button-press.

Procurement pipeline

STEP 1 · $1,500

Prototype sampling

Buy 5–10 developer units across target OEMs. Reverse the JSON payload structure and validate the full ingestion loop in the dev environment.

STEP 2

Factory selection

Only factories holding an active Medical CE Mark qualify — Moroccan regulation mirrors European frameworks, so CE dramatically accelerates import approval.

STEP 3 · MOQ ~500 · first batch 50 ($6,000)

White-label order

Factory prints our logo on shells and boxes, and flashes firmware so devices transmit to our AWS IoT endpoints instead of the factory cloud.

STEP 4 · ADDED

Connectivity contract

Sign an M2M SIM agreement with a Moroccan operator (Maroc Telecom / Orange / inwi) or a global roaming-SIM provider. Verify LTE-M/NB-IoT coverage on real routes patients travel — and require devices with 2G/4G Cat-1 fallback, because low-power IoT coverage outside major cities cannot be assumed.

PLAIN TERMS

OEM / ODM / white-label: a factory that already designed and certified the device manufactures it with your brand on it. You skip years of hardware R&D and certification.

MOQ: Minimum Order Quantity — the smallest batch a factory will produce for a custom order.

M2M SIM: a SIM card plan built for machines, not people — tiny data bundles, fleet management dashboard, per-device pricing.

SECTION 05

Regulatory & Compliance ADDED

This is the section clinics' lawyers will read first. Health data is the most protected data category in Moroccan law, and it must be handled before the first patient is enrolled.

AreaRequirementAction
Personal data — Law 09-08 / CNDPProcessing health data in Morocco requires prior authorization (not just notification) from the CNDP, the national data-protection commission.File the CNDP authorization dossier in month 1 with local counsel. Budgeted in the $10K legal line.
Data residency & transfersSending Moroccan health data to foreign servers is a cross-border transfer needing CNDP approval.Deploy in an approved AWS region, encrypt end-to-end, pseudonymize patient identity (device ID ↔ patient name mapping stays inside the clinic's system where feasible), and cover transfers explicitly in the CNDP filing.
Medical device import — DMPWearables must be registered/approved for import by the Ministry of Health's device directorate; the CE mark accelerates but does not replace this.The OEM's CE technical file + a local importer of record. Confirm whether we or the distributor act as importer.
Liability positioningThe platform must not be construed as practicing medicine or guaranteeing intervention.Contracts state: the platform transmits data and alerts; all clinical decisions and response duties remain with the clinic. Carry professional liability + cyber insurance.
Patient consentEach patient must consent to remote monitoring and data processing.Standard bilingual (Arabic/French) consent form signed at discharge, provided by us as part of the clinic onboarding kit.
WHY THIS ORDER MATTERS

CNDP authorization can take months. Filing it in parallel with prototyping — not after — is what protects the 6-month go-live target. Nothing in the pilot may touch a real patient's identity until authorization is granted; early pilots can run on volunteer/staff data if needed.

DevOps cost-suppression mandates

Graviton everywhere: every EKS and MSK instance runs ARM-based AWS Graviton chips, cutting compute cost ~20% vs x86. Version hygiene: CI/CD alerts (GitHub Actions) track Kubernetes versions, because letting EKS fall into "Extended Support" spikes the control-plane fee from $73 to $438/month overnight.

Added: costs the original plan under-counts

ADDED  Budget lines to add to the operating model: M2M SIM data (~$1–3/device/month → ~$250–750/mo at 250 active patients), SMS alert costs (Twilio/Infobip per-message fees), device attrition (assume 5–10% of the fleet lost/damaged/not returned per year — build a deposit or replacement fee into clinic contracts), insurance premiums, and logistics/cleaning for devices rotating between patients (each returned device must be sanitized, battery-checked and re-kitted).

PLAIN TERMS

Multi-AZ: the system runs simultaneously in physically separate data-center zones, so a single facility failure can't take patient monitoring offline.

Gross profit here = revenue − cloud bill. It is not net profit: the sales retainer, SIM data, SMS, insurance and the founder's own salary come out of it next.

Go-To-Market & Sales Execution ORIGINAL

Selling to individual patients is a low-margin trap. We sell to the people who control the beds: directors and chief cardiologists of high-end private clinics in Casablanca and Rabat.

Upfront cost to the hospital: $0. No licenses, no hardware purchases. The clinic bills the patient, keeps the majority of the margin, and reduces its own readmission liability. The product sells the clinic a new revenue line, not a cost.

The "Trojan Horse" pilot strategy

MOVE 1 · THE OPENER

Hire the relationship, not just a salesperson

A well-networked Moroccan medical-device distributor on a $2,000/mo retainer + commission. They already have the clinic directors' phone numbers and walk past gatekeepers.

MOVE 2 · THE RISK-FREE OFFER

Free 30-day trial, 10 devices

Target 5 prominent private cardiology clinics: "Deploy these on your next 10 discharging patients. Pay nothing. Just watch my dashboard."

MOVE 3 · THE HOOK

Let the system catch its first event

Within 30 days across 10 monitored patients, the platform will flag an anomaly — an arrhythmia, an oxygen dip — and the clinic intervenes early. Once the dashboard demonstrably protects a patient, the trial converts to a paid contract.

TARGET

10 clinic contracts within 6 months of market entry

≈ $17.5–20K MRR, establishing operational cash-flow profitability in the first half-year.

ADDED  Honest caveat to present alongside the hook: a clinically significant event within any given 30-day/10-patient trial is likely but not guaranteed. The backup conversion argument is operational: earlier discharges, freed beds, and documented monitoring coverage — value that exists even in a quiet month. Never promise a "save."

Foundations

Incorporate (SARL), open banking, file CNDP dossier, order OEM samples, build ingestion pipeline (IoT Core → Kafka → alert engine) against sample devices, sign M2M SIM agreement, hire the distributor/closer.

Product hardening

Clinic dashboard v1 (French UI), SMS escalation ladder, dead-man switch, coverage field tests, place the 50-unit white-label order, prepare bilingual consent forms and contracts.

MONTHS 4–6

Pilot wave

Deliver free 30-day trials to 5 cardiology clinics (10 devices each, staggered). Weekly threshold-tuning with each chief cardiologist. Collect testimonials and event logs.

MONTHS 6–9

Convert & expand

Convert pilots to paid PPPM contracts, reorder hardware from cash flow, extend the pilot playbook to the next 5 clinics, first contract hire (support/logistics).

MONTHS 9–12

Consolidate

Target: 10 signed clinics, ~250 active patients, ≈$17.5K MRR. Publish a case study (readmission + bed-turnover data) — the sales weapon for year 2 and the evidence base for any future fundraise.

KPIs — What We Measure ADDED

% of free trials converting to contracts. Target ≥ 60%. Below that, the pitch or the product needs surgery.

Time-to-alert acknowledgement

Minutes from threshold breach to a nurse tapping "seen." The number that proves the product works.

CLINICAL VALUE

False-positive rate

% of alerts dismissed as non-events. High = alert fatigue = churn. Tuned per patient.

OPERATIONS

% of devices transmitting in the last hour; device loss/damage rate; average turnaround time between patients.

OPERATIONS

Total cloud + SIM + SMS bill ÷ active patients. Should fall every quarter as the platform scales.
